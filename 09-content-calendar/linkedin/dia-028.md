Audit logs in clinical AI are not a compliance checkbox. They are the forensic primitive that determines whether a translation error becomes a legal liability or a defended clinical decision. Here is the technical difference.

Most clinical AI products log after the fact. A translation is delivered, a clinician acts on it, and at the end of the day the system writes a summary to the audit log. The log is a record of what happened. It is not a control on what happens.

The Orquor Clinical audit log operates on a different principle: write before act. The orchestrator writes every routing decision to the audit log before the resulting action is taken. The sequence is:

1. Verifier suite runs against the MT output.
2. If verifiers pass: the orchestrator writes "verified, AI route, suite v1.7, verifiers V1-V60 passed" to the audit log. Then the translation is delivered to the clinician's screen.
3. If verifiers fail at weight 5: the orchestrator writes "escalated to human, reason V2 dosage mismatch, output blocked" to the audit log. Then the interpreter is notified. The blocked translation never reaches the screen.

The write-before-act principle means the audit log is a causal record, not a retrospective summary. If the system crashes between the write and the act, the log contains the decision but the act was never taken. If the system is compromised between the write and the act, the forensic discrepancy is detectable.

The legal posture this creates is distinct from the industry default.

**Default posture.** A translation error occurs. The patient is harmed. Discovery begins. The vendor produces audit logs that show the system operated normally. The plaintiff's expert asks: "Did the log capture the routing decision before or after the translation was delivered?" The vendor cannot answer because the log was written after delivery. The question of whether the system could have intercepted the error is unanswerable. The vendor's liability posture is weakened.

**Write-before-act posture.** A translation error occurs. The patient is harmed. Discovery begins. The audit log shows that at timestamp 14:32:07.441, verifier V2 failed on dosage, the orchestrator escalated to the human interpreter, and the corrected translation was delivered at 14:32:09.882. The error was intercepted. The log proves it. The standard of care was met. The vendor's liability posture is strengthened conclusively.

**With cryptographic timestamping** (OpenTimestamps, anchoring the log hash to the Bitcoin blockchain): the audit log entry is provably immutable. The timestamps cannot be backdated. The log cannot be tampered with after the fact. A regulator or a court can independently verify that the log entry was created at the claimed time and has not been modified.

The audit log is not a compliance artifact. It is the technical foundation of the company's liability posture. Building it correctly — write-before-act, cryptographically timestamped, versioned with the verifier suite — costs more at the architecture stage and less at the litigation stage.

If you are building clinical AI and your audit log is an afterthought, fix it now. The cost of retrofitting is higher than the cost of building it right from line one.
