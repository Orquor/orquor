# LinkedIn — Día 16

**Format**: response to the field. **Tone**: receptive but firm where it matters.

---

The whitepaper has been out for nine days. Here is what the early readers have said and where they are right or wrong.

**Strongest agreement** — "publishing the spec openly is the correct move." Several readers in industry expected us to either patent ACTO or trademark the term. We have done neither. The reaction has been almost uniformly positive among independent clinical informaticists and regulatory researchers, and almost uniformly skeptical among VC-stage advisors. The skeptics ask: "how do you defend the moat?" The answer in the whitepaper is on page 8: the moat is implementation quality and dataset accumulation, not vocabulary control.

**Strongest disagreement** — "the WER benchmark numbers are too good to be true." Several readers have written in to challenge the 4.1% medical WER on ER specialty. The challenges fall into two buckets. The first is methodological: how was the evaluation set constructed? Fair question, addressed in Section 5 of the whitepaper. We are publishing the eval set openly in 2026 for independent replication. The second is statistical: 4.1% on 500 utterances has a confidence interval of plus or minus roughly 1.8 percentage points. Also fair. The larger benchmark with 1,000 utterances per specialty will tighten the interval.

**Largest miss** — Section 6, regulatory posture, is too brief. A reader at a US health system noted that we treat HIPAA, GDPR, Law 29733, and LGPD in two paragraphs each. They are right. A subsequent paper or extended appendix will treat each regulatory framework with the depth it deserves.

**Quote of the week** — from a regulator who emailed in response to the whitepaper: "This is the first time I have read a technical paper from an AI company in healthcare that did not require me to translate from marketing language back into engineering language." That is the highest compliment I will receive this year.

**Where I changed my mind** — I had budgeted 60 days for the term "ACTO" to be adopted by anyone outside Orquor. It happened in nine. The implication is that the next term we coin should be more carefully timed. A category vocabulary is a one-shot weapon. Fire it once, fire it well, then defend with implementation quality.
