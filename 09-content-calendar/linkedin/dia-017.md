The Cooperative Inference Stack — a deeper look at how Orquor coordinates AI components and human specialists into a single pipeline.

A traditional AI-translation product treats the model as the system. The model takes audio in, produces text out. Human review, if it happens, is bolted on the side as a quality-assurance afterthought.

The Cooperative Inference Stack inverts that frame. The orchestrator is the system. The AI components and the human interpreter are interchangeable resources that the orchestrator dispatches based on what each utterance requires.

Five concrete behaviors illustrate the difference.

**One — confidence-routed dispatch.** Every utterance enters the orchestrator with metadata about specialty, register, urgency, and patient consent. The orchestrator routes the audio to the ASR. The ASR returns a transcript with per-token confidence. If average confidence is above 0.92, the transcript proceeds to MT. If below 0.85, the orchestrator wakes a human interpreter listening on a side channel; they confirm or correct the transcript in real time.

**Two — verifier-driven retries.** When the MT output fails a verifier, the orchestrator does not silently degrade. The failure signal becomes feedback to a second-pass MT. If the second pass also fails, the utterance escalates to the human interpreter with the failure context surfaced. The human sees not just the audio but the specific reason the AI failed.

**Three — register lock-in across a session.** Once a session begins with the Formal/Clinical register selected, the orchestrator enforces consistency. If an utterance produces a translation in a different register, it fails verifier V5 and is regenerated. The clinician never sees register drift mid-session.

**Four — graceful degradation on infrastructure failure.** If the primary GPU is unreachable, the orchestrator detects the timeout, routes the session entirely to the human interpreter pool, and flags the session in the audit log as "human-only mode". The clinician notices a higher latency floor but the session does not break.

**Five — audit log as a write target, not an afterthought.** Every routing decision the orchestrator makes is written to the audit log before the resulting action is taken. The order matters: the system writes its decision, then acts on it. If the actor never reads back the write, the decision is unbuffered and can be reconstructed forensically.

This is the architecture that makes Orquor different from "an AI translation product". The model is a component. The system is the orchestration discipline that decides what the model does and what to do when it fails.
