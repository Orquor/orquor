I shipped five versions of the same product before I named the company.

The folder names on my laptop tell the story:
ITERAMED.
ITERAMED ULTIMA VERSION.
ITERAMED ULTIMA VERSION 2 GPUS.
ITERAMED VERSION FINAL MIG A100.
ITERAMEDGOLD.

Anyone who has built anything technical knows that "FINAL" never means final. "GOLD" is the name you give a version when you are tired of naming versions.

Each iteration taught me something that the last one couldn't.

V1 taught me the architecture matters less than the deployment context.
V2 taught me that a clever model is worthless if a hospital cannot defend its outputs in an adverse-event review.
V3 taught me that scale is a function of GPU partitioning, not raw FLOPS.
V4 taught me that the bottleneck is the human verifier loop, not the AI.
V5 — the one I called GOLD — taught me to stop building until I had named the category we were building inside of.

That last lesson is the reason the company is now called Orquor. And it's the reason the next thing I'm publishing isn't a product update. It's a category definition. A whitepaper that names what we have been quietly building and invites everyone else to operate in the same vocabulary.

Building is iterative. Naming is categorical.
