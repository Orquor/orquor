Why LATAM is the right place to build healthcare AI for the Spanish-speaking world. Not the cheap place. The right place.

The cheap argument is true and uninteresting. Senior engineering salaries in Lima are 30 percent of Bay Area equivalents. That is a margin lever, not a strategic position.

The strategic arguments are five, and they compound.

**One — vocabulary neutrality.** Clinical Spanish in Lima maps cleanly to clinical Spanish in Bogotá, San José, Mexico City, Miami, Buenos Aires. Mexican clinical Spanish does not map as cleanly into Argentine. Argentine does not map cleanly into Caribbean. Lima sits at the geographic and linguistic center of the LATAM clinical Spanish space. A model trained on Lima data generalizes; a model trained on the linguistic edges does not.

**Two — regulatory accessibility.** Peru's Authority for Personal Data Protection is approachable. A founder can request a technical briefing and receive a response within two weeks. The same request to the OCR at HHS in the US takes months and requires formal counsel. For a company in the regulatory-design phase, dialogue with the regulator at design time is worth more than any quantity of post-deployment defensibility.

**Three — talent gravity.** PUCP, UTEC, UPC, UPC Lima — these schools have produced senior engineers who have shipped at Meta, Anthropic, Google, Stripe, Cloudflare. Most of them want to come back. Few of the world's other early-stage software cities can say the same about reverse migration.

**Four — patient market alignment.** The Spanish-speaking diaspora in the United States is 62 million people. Their healthcare is consistently underserved on the language axis. A company built in Lima that serves both LATAM healthcare and US-Hispanic healthcare has a market that no purely-US company can match on cultural fit, and no purely-LATAM company can match on regulatory rigor.

**Five — sovereign AI tailwind.** Latin American governments — Peru, Colombia, Mexico, Brazil — are starting to fund domestic AI infrastructure as a matter of policy. Innóvate Perú, ProInnovate, BID Lab, the Brazilian PROFLOR program. The funding window for a company positioned as "sovereign AI infrastructure for the Spanish-speaking healthcare market" is open in 2026 in a way that was not true in 2024.

The next ten years of LATAM software are not going to be outsourcing for the US. They are going to be sovereign capability for the Spanish-speaking world. Orquor is the bet that this transition has a beginning, a middle, and a winner.

We are not building in LATAM because it is cheaper. We are building in LATAM because it is closer to the customer, closer to the language, closer to the regulator, and closer to the future of where this capability needs to live.
