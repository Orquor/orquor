The dirty secret of healthcare-AI vendors in 2026: most of them have never written a verifier.

Here's what I mean by a verifier.

A verifier is a Python function that runs after an AI system produces an output. It checks one specific property of that output. Does the translated sentence contain the same numeric dosage as the source? Does it use the canonical Spanish term for the controlled substance the doctor named? Does it not contain the name of a person who should not appear in the clinical record?

Each verifier returns pass or fail with a structured reason and a signed weight. Catastrophic violations weigh -5. Mild stylistic issues weigh +1. The weighted sum determines whether the output is delivered, escalated to a human, or quarantined.

This is not new computer science. It is the methodology that the most demanding agent-evaluation projects in the industry already use, including ones I have built rubrics and verifiers for at scale.

What is new is bringing that discipline to clinical AI.

When a vendor tells you "our AI is 95% accurate," ask them: 95% accurate on what verifier suite? Show me the failing 5%. Tell me which failures are catastrophic versus stylistic. Show me the weighted-sum gating policy.

If they cannot answer, they are not running a verifier suite. They are running an unaccountable model with a confidence-interval marketing pitch.

The Orquor Clinical verifier suite has ~60 verifiers across nine clinical specialties. It runs before every translation reaches the screen. The audit log records every pass and every fail.
