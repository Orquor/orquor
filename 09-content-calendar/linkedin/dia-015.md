**Format**: first-prospect retrospective. **Tone**: candid, learnings-oriented.

What I learned in my first 5 prospect conversations as Orquor.

I cultivated these prospects for an average of 41 days before any of them sat down with me on a video call. The cultivation was ambient — comments on their LinkedIn posts, sharing the ACTO whitepaper, attending two industry events where they were also present. No outbound DMs. No cold emails.

This week the first five sat down. Here is what surprised me.

**One.** The pricing question came up second, not first. The first question in three out of five conversations was "where is the data processed". The second was "show me the audit log format". The third was "what is your liability posture". Pricing did not enter until question four. The prospect's mental model is risk first, capability second, price third.

**Two.** The decision-maker is not the CIO. In four out of five, the conversation was led by the Director of International Patient Services or the CMO. The CIO joined the second call. The procurement model in medical-tourism clinics is fundamentally driven by the international-patient PnL owner, not by the technology budget. This changed how I structure the second conversation.

**Three.** They want to see your worst hour. Two prospects asked: "Can we test against an actual recording from last Friday's ER?" That is a brutal test set. We accepted in both cases. Performance held — 4.3% WER vs 4.1% in our internal benchmark, well within tolerance. But the willingness to be tested against the worst hour is itself the signal that gets prospects to take you seriously.

**Four.** They cite competitors I have not heard of. Two prospects referenced regional competitors I had not mapped. One is a Costa Rican company called LinguaSalud (privately held, no online footprint that I had found in OSINT scans). Another is a Mexican startup called Intermed.AI that does live captioning for clinical calls. The lesson: my competitive map is incomplete until I have had 10 prospect conversations.

**Five.** The whitepaper carried the conversation. In all five calls, the prospect had read the whitepaper. Three of them quoted specific sections back to me. The fourth had marked it up and sent me his copy with highlights. The whitepaper is not marketing material — it is the discovery vehicle. Without it, the first conversation would have been spent explaining the category. With it, the first conversation was spent on their specific use case.

Building authority through depth, not gloss, is working.
