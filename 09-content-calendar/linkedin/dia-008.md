Notice something in every AI demo from healthcare-AI companies in 2026: the demos use English.

The conversation is calm. The pronunciation is broadcast-grade. The medical vocabulary is intermediate — "headache", "blood pressure", "shortness of breath". Never "subarachnoid hemorrhage". Never "ventricular tachycardia with pulse". Never "0.05 mg per kilogram of midazolam IV push".

There is a reason. The hard vocabulary breaks the demo.

A standard Whisper Large v3 model goes from 6% Word Error Rate on conversational English to 11.8% on US clinical vocabulary, to 18% on Spanish neurology dictation. The vendor showing you a polished demo with "headache" is not lying — they just selected the inputs where the model behaves.

When we run an Orquor Clinical pilot against a hospital's real recordings, the test set is brutal: surgical handoffs, pharmacy phone consults, ER triage at 02:00 AM in regional Spanish, post-op rounds in operating-room slang. That is the dataset that defines whether the system works in a real hospital.

If a vendor will not run a head-to-head against your worst hour of audio, that is the answer to whether they should be allowed into your operating room.
