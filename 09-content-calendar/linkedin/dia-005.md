The hospital procurement question that nobody asks AI vendors out loud:

"Show me the per-utterance audit log of your last 100 sessions. Show me which verifier failed and why."

99% of vendors cannot answer.

Here is what is in a real per-utterance audit log:

1. The session ID and the utterance ID — sequential, immutable
2. A reference to the encrypted audio blob (the blob itself is not in the log)
3. The ASR transcript with per-token confidence scores
4. The translated output with the register that was applied
5. The verifier suite result: every test that ran, every pass, every fail with structured reason
6. A timestamp from an external cryptographic timestamping authority — RFC 3161 or OpenTimestamps anchored to Bitcoin
7. The signature of the entry by the orchestrator's hardware key

That last layer matters. The hardware-key signature is verifiable by any external party without trusting the operator. Five years from now in an adverse-event review, a hospital can produce its complete audit log and an independent auditor can verify it against the Bitcoin blockchain without the operator's participation.

That's what "defensible" means in healthcare AI. Not a marketing word. A cryptographic property.

If your translation vendor's audit log is "session start: 14:32. session end: 14:38." — that is not an audit log. That is metadata.

Ask the harder question on your next RFP.
