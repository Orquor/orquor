What HIPAA actually requires for AI-mediated medical interpretation. The short answer is "more than most vendors think". The long answer is below.

**Administrative safeguards (45 CFR § 164.308).** A Business Associate Agreement is mandatory before any PHI is exchanged. Most vendors have a template. Few have considered the specific clauses needed for AI processing: model retraining boundaries, dataset retention windows, deidentification standards, breach notification windows tightened for AI failure modes. If your vendor's BAA does not mention these, the BAA is generic, not AI-aware.

**Physical safeguards (45 CFR § 164.310).** The GPU hosting matters. Hyperstack and shared cloud providers may not be willing to sign a BAA for the underlying hardware. The vendor that processes your PHI on a shared cloud without an upstream BAA is in technical violation. Confirm upstream BAAs as part of the procurement check.

**Technical safeguards (45 CFR § 164.312).** Three sub-requirements that are routinely under-implemented:

(a) Access controls — § 164.312(a). Unique user identification, automatic logoff, encryption-decryption controls. The encryption part is widely implemented; the automatic logoff is widely ignored. AI sessions that stay open across clinicians without re-authentication fail this control.

(b) Audit controls — § 164.312(b). Hardware, software, and procedural mechanisms that record and examine activity in systems containing PHI. The plural in "mechanisms" is intentional. A single application log is not enough. Hardware-rooted audit (TPM-attested or HSM-signed) is what survives technical scrutiny.

(c) Integrity — § 164.312(c). Electronic PHI must not be improperly altered or destroyed. Cryptographic timestamping (RFC 3161, OpenTimestamps) is the cleanest way to demonstrate this property. Without it, integrity is a procedural claim, not a technical fact.

**Breach notification (45 CFR § 164.410).** A breach of unsecured PHI must be reported to HHS within 60 days. For an AI vendor, "unsecured" has a specific meaning — PHI is secured if it is encrypted to the standards in NIST SP 800-111 (at rest) and TLS 1.2+ (in transit). PHI processed in confidential-computing enclaves (H100 confidential mode, Intel TDX, AMD SEV-SNP) goes beyond the floor — it is mathematically inaccessible to the operator, which changes the breach analysis materially.

A hospital procurement team that asks the AI vendor "are you HIPAA compliant?" is asking the wrong question. The right question is: walk me through your compliance posture on each of the four pillars above, and show me the evidence.

If the vendor cannot, they are not HIPAA compliant. They are HIPAA-aspirational.
