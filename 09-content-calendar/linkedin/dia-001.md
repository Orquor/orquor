Why "we use AI for translation" is no longer enough in healthcare.

In 2026, the standard answer when a hospital CIO asks how their interpretation service works is some version of: "we have a vendor; the vendor has AI; it works most of the time."

Most of the time. In a setting where 10 mg of morphine and 0.1 mg of morphine are 100× apart, "most of the time" is a euphemism for "we will be fine until we are not, and when we are not, we have no evidence of what happened."

There is a category that should exist by now and doesn't. It combines real-time machine translation with a verifier suite that checks every output against outcome-based tests before that output reaches the screen. Every session is logged with cryptographic timestamping. Every consequential decision — register selection, fallback to a human interpreter, escalation — is auditable years later.

We call it Auditable Clinical Translation Orchestration. ACTO. We are publishing the technical specification openly so the industry can rise together rather than competing on opacity.
