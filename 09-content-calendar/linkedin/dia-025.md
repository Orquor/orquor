First internal pilot data from the cooperative inference stack: 14 sessions across three specialties, one interpreter, 1,847 utterances. Here is what the numbers say, and what they do not say.

**The context.** This is an internal pilot, not a clinical deployment. Sessions were simulated with de-identified recordings from a partner clinic. The goal was not to demonstrate clinical safety — that requires a prospective trial with live patients and IRB approval, which is the next step. The goal was to measure the routing behavior of the orchestrator under realistic workload.

**The numbers.**

- Total utterances processed: 1,847
- Routed entirely through AI (no verifier failures): 1,582 (85.6%)
- Routed through AI with verifier-triggered retry (second-pass MT succeeded): 141 (7.6%)
- Escalated to human interpreter: 124 (6.7%)
- Of escalated utterances, corrected by interpreter: 118 (95.2%)
- Of escalated utterances, confirmed as correct by interpreter (verifier false positive): 6 (4.8%)

**What this means.** The orchestrator intercepted 124 utterances that the verifier suite flagged as potentially erroneous. The interpreter corrected 118 of them. The verifier suite's precision on the escalation decision was 95.2%. Put differently: when the verifier suite said "this translation needs a human", it was right 95% of the time.

**The 6 false positives.** All six were in the ER specialty, specifically in utterances where the patient used non-standard colloquial Spanish for anatomical terms. The verifier V9 (terminology registry compliance) flagged the colloquial terms as unrecognized. The interpreter confirmed the terms were contextually correct. This is a training-data gap in the terminology registry, not a verifier logic error. The fix is expanding the registry to include regional colloquial variants.

**What this does NOT mean.** A 1,847-utterance internal pilot does not generalize. The sessions were simulated, not live. The interpreter knew the sessions were simulated. The specialties were limited to ER, internal medicine, and family practice — no surgery, no oncology, no psychiatry. The next step is a prospective trial under IRB with live patients in at least five specialties.

**The cost per corrected utterance.** In this pilot, the interpreter handled 124 utterances in 14 sessions — approximately 9 escalations per session. At the current retainer rate, the effective cost per corrected utterance was USD 1.85. Extrapolating to a full clinical load of 60 encounters per day, the interpreter would handle approximately 50–60 escalated utterances per day, at a daily cost of USD 110–130. The hospital's total interpretation cost would drop approximately 40% from the current VRI model. The interpreter's effective hourly rate would rise approximately 35%.

The pilot numbers are directionally consistent with the model in the ACTO whitepaper. Directional consistency is not proof. The prospective trial is the proof.
