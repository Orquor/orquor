**What worked beyond expectation.**

Publishing the whitepaper openly was the highest-leverage decision of the three weeks. Inbound from people who would not have responded to outbound is more than the entire pre-launch pipeline. The category vocabulary propagated in nine days, not the sixty I had budgeted.

The technical-depth posts outperformed the marketing-tone posts by 4 to 6 times engagement. Every time I put a Python function on screen or a benchmark number, engagement spiked. Every time I posted abstract vision, engagement was thin.

The interpreter-direct positioning post on Day 19 generated more thoughtful replies than any post in the run. The certified medical interpreter community has been talking past AI vendors for two years; we are the first one that talked to them.

**What worked at the level I expected.**

Cultivation discipline on prospects. Every conversation that landed this month was the result of 30+ days of ambient engagement, not cold outreach. The same discipline that works in HUMINT tradecraft works in B2B sales, exactly as the playbook predicted.

The hybrid recording setup — iPhone 15 Pro, Rode Wireless ME, two affordable lights — produced video quality indistinguishable from a USD 12,000 setup. Production polish does not move the needle in this category. Substance does.

**What did not work.**

LinkedIn carousels. Engagement dropped 40 percent vs text-first posts. The audience here wants paragraphs, not slides.

The first podcast guesting attempt landed on a generic "AI in healthcare" podcast that did not let me get to the specifics. Lesson: pre-screen podcasts for whether they will let the conversation get technical enough to differentiate us.

The first attempt at sponsoring a small industry newsletter for awareness did not convert. ROI on broad-awareness spend at this stage is invisible. Pulling that budget back into product and into recruiting one technical advisor with the right network.

**Where I have changed my mind in three weeks.**

I had assumed the first conversations would be with hospital CIOs. They were with regulators. The procurement signal flows from regulator attention back to CIO attention, not the other way around. Different game, different motion. Adjusting accordingly.

I had assumed the second product to ship would be Orquor Triage (telehealth async). It is more likely to be the Orquor Telehealth API — the highest-synergy lift from existing infrastructure, the fastest time to revenue. Triage shifts to month 8 instead of month 6.

**What is next.**

Week 4 to 6: first hospital pilot LOI signed and public. Whitepaper extended appendix on regulatory framework. YouTube episodes 5 through 7 building toward the audit-log walkthrough that I have been promising. Orquor Academy applications opening Day 60.

Operating in public has been the right move. Three weeks in, I would not change a thing about how we launched.
