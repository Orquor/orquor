**What the numbers do not capture.**

Two competitors began using "audit log" in their marketing within days of the whitepaper going live. The vocabulary is propagating. ACTO is becoming a reference point before the product is generally available. This is category creation working as designed.

A regulator in a LATAM country requested a technical briefing on the ACTO compliance posture. Not a sales call. A briefing. The relationship compounds differently than a procurement conversation. It creates the procurement conversation downstream.

Two certified medical interpreters applied to join the Orquor interpreter pool because of the Day 19 post that explicitly addressed their community. The positioning of interpreter-as-irreplaceable-layer, not interpreter-as-displaced-worker, is working.

**What I got wrong this month.**

I underestimated the speed of memetic propagation. I budgeted 60 days for ACTO to be adopted outside Orquor. It happened in 9. The next term we coin will be timed more deliberately.

I overestimated the importance of production polish. Every post with a Python function or a benchmark number outperformed posts with vision statements. The audience in clinical AI does not want gloss. It wants evidence. I knew this intellectually. Operating in public taught me it viscerally.

I misjudged the procurement signal flow. I assumed the first inbound conversations would be with hospital CIOs. They were with regulators and clinical informaticists. The signal flows from regulator attention to CIO attention, not the reverse. The pipeline motion is different than what I planned for, and the adjustment is underway.

**What I got right.**

Publishing the ACTO whitepaper openly, without patent or trademark, was the correct strategic decision. The inbound from people who would not have responded to outbound exceeds the entire pre-launch pipeline.

Building authority through depth rather than gloss is working. The technical posts — verifier design, audit log architecture, pilot data — generate the highest-quality conversations. The posts that try to be accessible to everyone generate the shallowest engagement.

The cooperative inference positioning — interpreter as high-value resource at the top of the escalation stack — is the most differentiated message we have. It resonates with interpreters, clinicians, and regulators. It alienates exactly the AI-only vendors we are positioning against.

- YouTube episodes 5–8: verifier walkthrough, interpreter Camila guest, LATAM sovereign AI story, audit log deep-dive with regulatory attorney.

Operating in public for 30 days has confirmed the thesis: technical specificity over polish, evidence over vision, depth over reach.
