Human-in-the-loop in clinical AI is treated as a checkbox. Most vendors ship an AI system and add a "review queue" that a human checks at the end of the day. The human is not in the loop. The human is after the loop.

The cooperative inference stack at Orquor is not a review queue. It is a routing system. The human interpreter operates inside the loop, not after it. The difference is not cosmetic. It is architectural.

Here is what "after the loop" looks like in practice. The AI processes the session end-to-end. The output is written to the EHR. Twenty-four hours later, a quality reviewer opens a dashboard and flags three translations as questionable. The corrections are applied to the record, but the clinician who acted on the original translation has already made a clinical decision based on incorrect information. The loop is closed too late.

Here is what "inside the loop" looks like. The orchestrator receives an utterance. It routes to the ASR and MT pipeline. The verifier suite runs before the translation reaches the screen. If a verifier fails at weight 5, the orchestrator wakes a human interpreter — not after the session, but during it, on a side channel with the failure context surfaced. The interpreter corrects the translation. The correction feeds back into the pipeline. The corrected translation reaches the clinician's screen. The entire cycle, from verifier failure to corrected output, targets under 3 seconds.

The architectural difference has three consequences.

**Consequence 1 — The clinician never sees an uncorrected catastrophic error.** In the after-the-loop model, the clinician sees the error and acts on it. In the inside-the-loop model, the error is intercepted before it reaches the screen.

**Consequence 2 — The interpreter's work is focused.** In the after-the-loop model, the interpreter reviews entire sessions and finds errors in 5% of utterances. In the inside-the-loop model, the interpreter is invoked only when the verifier suite has already identified a specific failure. The interpreter spends 100% of their time on consequential corrections, not on scanning clean output.

**Consequence 3 — The audit log captures the routing decision.** Every dispatch from AI to human is a logged event with a timestamp, a verifier failure code, and the interpreter's correction. In the after-the-loop model, the review queue is a separate system from the AI pipeline, and the audit trail is fragmented across two logs.

The term "human-in-the-loop" has been diluted by every vendor adding a review button to their product. The precise term for what we are building is cooperative inference with real-time escalation. The human is not a safety net. The human is the irreplaceable top layer of a routing architecture designed around the fact that AI fails in predictable patterns, and those patterns can be intercepted.
