# X / Twitter — Día 7 Thread

**Format**: 6-tweet personal-story thread. **Hook**: a small embarrassing observation that opens a real lesson.

---

**1/**
The moment I realized iteramed.sbs was not enough.

I was in a sales conversation with a hospital procurement director. She was reading our materials live. She paused at the URL.

"Is the dot s-b-s a typo?"

That single question changed how I think about everything we do.

**2/**
A TLD is not a technical detail. It is a credibility signal that travels in front of every other piece of evidence about us. A hospital procurement director who has signed contracts with LanguageLine and Cloudbreak does not have a mental model that includes dot-sbs domains.

We were filtered out before our argument had a chance to land.

**3/**
The lesson is broader than domains.

Every consumer-tier shortcut we take in our infrastructure — a free SSL on a cheap hosting tier, a generic placeholder logo, a Calendly link with someone else's brand on it — costs us a small fraction of credibility. The fractions compound.

**4/**
The opposite is also true.

A dot-com or dot-health on a real registrar. A self-hosted blog with our writing. A logo that was designed deliberately even if simply. A pitch deck that reads like the company is going to be worth USD 100M, not USD 1M. Each fraction compounds.

**5/**
Two weeks after that conversation, we are operating under Orquor. Domain belt registered. Brand identity defined. Whitepaper in production. The same procurement director who paused at dot-sbs returned my email within 90 minutes when I sent her the whitepaper from research-at-orquor-dot-com.

The signal traveled in front. We had not changed our product. We had changed what arrived first.

**6/**
If you are early-stage and tempted to skip the credibility-signal layer because the product is great — do not.

The signal arrives before the product. Build the signal as carefully as the product.
