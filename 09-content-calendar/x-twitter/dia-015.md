# X / Twitter — Día 15 Thread

**Format**: 6-tweet thread on outcome-based verifiers. **Hook**: a sharp question.

---

**1/**
Most clinical AI vendors cannot tell you, with a straight face, what happens when their model produces a wrong translation at 03:14 AM in a rural ER.

The answer in most systems: nothing. The error reaches the clinician. The clinician acts on it. The error is discovered later, or not at all.

Here is the alternative.

**2/**
A verifier suite is a set of deterministic functions that check properties of a translation output before it reaches the screen.

Not a model. Not a heuristic. A function that returns pass/fail with a weight.

When a verifier fails, the system blocks the translation and escalates to a human. Before the error reaches the clinician.

**3/**
The design rules:

→ Atomic: one check, one verifier. No composites.
→ Weighted: catastrophic = 5, moderate = 3, stylistic = 1.
→ Negative verifiers required: detect what should NOT appear.
→ Versioned: every verifier version is in the audit log.

The same four rules apply whether you build for translation, summarization, or clinical decision support.

**4/**
The calibration method: clinical expert panel → anchor to adverse event epidemiology → legal adjudication of boundary cases → prospective validation on held-out data.

Not a machine-learning problem. A clinical risk management problem.

**5/**
We calibrated 60 verifiers this way. The suite blocks 94% of catastrophic errors in internal validation. False-positive block rate: 2.1%.

The numbers are preliminary. The prospective trial with live patients is the proof. But the methodology is public. Any clinical AI vendor can adopt it.

**6/**
If you ship clinical AI without a verifier suite, you are shipping without a safety layer. The model will fail. The question is whether you detect the failure before the clinician acts on it, or after.
