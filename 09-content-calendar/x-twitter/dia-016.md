**1/**
"Human-in-the-loop" is the most misused term in clinical AI.

Most vendors: AI processes everything → output delivered → human reviews at end of day.

That is human-after-the-loop. The clinician has already acted on the output. The review is documentation, not interception.

**2/**
Cooperative inference with real-time escalation is different:

→ Verifier fails on an utterance
→ Orchestrator wakes human interpreter (not after session — during it)
→ Failure context surfaced to interpreter
→ Interpreter corrects in <3 seconds
→ Corrected output reaches clinician

The human is inside the loop, not after it.

**3/**
Consequences:

→ Clinician never sees uncorrected catastrophic error
→ Interpreter spends 100% of time on consequential corrections, not scanning clean output
→ Every routing decision is in the audit log as a write-before-act event
→ The loop is closed in seconds, not hours

**4/**
The economic model: interpreter handles 50% fewer sessions at 1.6–1.8× per-session rate. Hospital total cost drops 35–45%. Interpreter hourly effective rate rises 30–40%.

Both sides win. The system designed to replace interpreters charges less and absorbs full volume. Verifier liability makes that architecture untenable.

**5/**
If you are building clinical AI and your "human-in-the-loop" is a review queue at the end of the day: you have not built human-in-the-loop. You have built human-after-the-loop.

The difference is architectural. Fix it before deployment, not after.
