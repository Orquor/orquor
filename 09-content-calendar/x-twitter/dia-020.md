**1/**
If you are a hospital procurement team evaluating clinical AI translation in 2026, here are the five questions your RFP should ask and probably does not.

**2/**
1. "Show me your verifier suite."

Not "how many languages do you support." Not "what is your accuracy."

Show me the deterministic functions that check translation output before it reaches the screen. How many verifiers? What are the weights? What is the false-positive block rate?

If the vendor cannot answer, they do not have a verifier suite.

**3/**
2. "Show me your audit log format for a single translation."

The log should capture: the routing decision, the verifier results, the timestamp, and whether the log was written before or after the output was delivered.

If the log is written after delivery, it is a retrospective summary, not a forensic primitive.

**4/**
3. "What is your write-before-act policy?"

If the vendor does not understand the question, they have not built their audit architecture correctly.

**5/**
4. "What is your interpreter escalation architecture?"

Is the interpreter inside the loop (real-time) or after the loop (end-of-day review)? What is the target latency from verifier failure to corrected output?

If the answer is "end-of-day review queue", the human is not in the loop.

**6/**
5. "Show me your liability posture for an undetected translation error."

What does the audit log prove? Can a regulator independently verify the timestamps? Is the log cryptographically anchored?

The vendor's answer to this question tells you whether they have thought about liability or whether they are hoping you do not ask.

Read the ACTO whitepaper. Write the RFP. Don't let the vendor write it for you.
