**1/**
5 reasons your hospital's current interpreter vendor cannot show you the audit log of last week's session.

If the vendor's answer to "show me the log" is anything other than a downloadable file in your hands within 5 minutes, the answer is in this thread.

**2/**
Reason 1: they don't have one.

Most VRI/OPI vendors retain session metadata — start, end, interpreter ID. They do not retain the audio. They do not retain the spoken content. The "audit log" they describe in the contract is a billing log, not a clinical record.

**3/**
Reason 2: they have it but it lives in the interpreter's notes.

The certified interpreter wrote a summary after the session. That summary is in a database somewhere. It is not verbatim. It does not capture the moments that matter most — register, hesitation, the times the interpreter asked the doctor to repeat.

**4/**
Reason 3: they have it but it is not cryptographically anchored.

The vendor can produce a transcript. The transcript was generated yesterday. Whether the transcript reflects what was actually said three months ago is a matter of vendor trust, not mathematical verification.

**5/**
Reason 4: they have it but the legal team will not release it.

The audit log exists. It is in a database. Releasing it to the customer requires sign-off from the vendor's GC, which is not coming. The hospital owns the data on paper; the vendor controls access in practice.

**6/**
Reason 5: they have it and will release it, but only the version that helps them.

The selective audit log. The version that is shown to a customer in a normal situation is not the same version that surfaces during a regulatory investigation. This is the worst version — the appearance of accountability without the substance.
