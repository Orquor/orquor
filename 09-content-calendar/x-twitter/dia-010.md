**3/**
Principle 1 — Verifiers evaluate outcomes, not traces.

A verifier checks that the translated output has a property. Not that the model "called this tool" or "followed this prompt path".

This makes verifiers robust to implementation changes. As models improve, verifiers stay valid.

**4/**
Principle 2 — Verifiers are atomic.

One check, one verifier. No conjunctions. No disjunctions.

A composite verifier that bundles dosage + units + drug-name is impossible to debug.

Split it. Three verifiers. Each weighted independently.

**5/**
Principle 3 — Verifiers are weighted explicitly.

Catastrophic violations: weight ±5.
Moderate: ±3.
Stylistic: ±1.

The orchestrator's escalation logic uses the weighted sum.

This is the difference between an "alert" and a "block decision".

**6/**
Principle 4 — Negative verifiers are required.

A suite of only-positive verifiers is incomplete. It cannot detect leakage of disallowed content.

Every Orquor verifier suite ships with at least one negative verifier (V4 in the paper: no excluded names).

**7/**
Principle 5 — Verifiers are versioned.

When a verifier changes, the version is in the audit log.

A translation verified under suite v1.2 is auditable as such even after the suite updates to v1.3.

The audit log becomes a forensic primitive, not just a metric.
