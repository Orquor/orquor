**1/**
Verifier methodology in 5 tweets. Adapted from frontier agent-evaluation research. Applied to clinical translation.

**2/**
Principle 1 — Outcome-based, not trace-based.

A verifier checks that the translated output has a property. Not that the model "called this tool" or "followed this prompt path".

This makes verifiers robust to implementation changes. Models improve. Verifiers stay valid.

**3/**
Principle 2 — Atomic, not composite.

One check, one verifier. A verifier that bundles dosage + units + drug-name is impossible to debug.

Split: V1 (dosage value), V2 (dosage unit), V3 (drug name). Three weights. Three failure messages. Independently auditable.

**4/**
Principle 3 — Weighted, not binary.

Catastrophic violations: weight ±5.
Moderate: ±3.
Stylistic: ±1.

The orchestrator's gating decision uses the weighted sum. The same gating policy is auditable: a regulator can verify why a translation was delivered or escalated.

**5/**
Principle 4 — Negative verifiers required.

A suite of only-positive verifiers is incomplete. It cannot detect leakage of disallowed content.

Every Orquor suite ships with at least one negative verifier (V4 in the whitepaper: no excluded names appear in the translation).
