**1/**
"Will AI replace medical interpreters?" is the wrong question.

The right question: what is the most efficient division of labor between AI and certified interpreters, given that catastrophic translation errors cost USD 350k–4.2M per case?

The answer is the cooperative inference stack.

**2/**
The AI handles structured high-frequency utterances — vitals, medication confirmation, basic anamnesis. These are the 70% of session volume where AI achieves clinical-grade accuracy.

**3/**
The human interpreter handles consequential moments — informed consent, family meetings, complex history, anything the verifier suite flags as uncertain. These are the 30% where the cost of error justifies the unit economics of a human.

**4/**
The orchestrator routes between them in real time. Confidence below threshold? Route to human. Verifier failed? Route to human. Specialty register requires cultural nuance? Route to human.

The interpreter is not on standby. The interpreter is the irreplaceable layer of a multi-tier system.

**5/**
Economics: hospital total cost drops 35–45%. Interpreter hourly rate rises 30–40%. Volume per interpreter drops. Pay per session rises. Both sides win.

The system designed to replace interpreters would charge less than market and absorb full volume. Verifier liability makes that architecture untenable.
