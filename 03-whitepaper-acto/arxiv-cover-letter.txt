SUMMARY FOR MODERATORS
================================================================================

This paper proposes "Auditable Clinical Translation Orchestration" (ACTO)
as a new technical category for real-time medical interpretation systems.
ACTO combines automated speech recognition, machine translation, and
human-in-the-loop interpreters into a pipeline where every output is verified
by deterministic outcome-based tests and recorded in a cryptographically
timestamped audit log.

The paper makes three contributions:

  1. A reference architecture for verifier-backed translation pipelines
     (Section 3), with five required components: orchestrator, ASR, MT,
     verifier suite, and cryptographic audit primitive.

  2. A verifier-design methodology (Section 4) adapted from agent-evaluation
     research, formalizing five principles for building deterministic test
     suites for clinical NLP output.

  3. A benchmark methodology (Section 5) with metrics (WER, COMET-22,
     verifier pass rate, latency, human escalation rate) and preliminary
     results from a 500-utterance evaluation set.

================================================================================
WHY THIS BELONGS ON ARXIV
================================================================================

This paper is a legitimate scientific contribution to the NLP community:

  - It identifies a categorical gap in the medical interpretation literature
    (the quality-cost-defensibility trilemma) and proposes a formal category
    to resolve it.

  - It contributes a methodology (verifier design for clinical translation)
    that is domain-independent and reusable by other researchers.

  - It proposes a standardized benchmark that enables apples-to-apples
    comparison of medical interpretation systems — a contribution to
    reproducible research in clinical NLP.

  - It includes preliminary empirical results (Table in Section 5.4) that,
    while explicitly labeled as non-definitive, demonstrate feasibility.

  - The paper cites prior work appropriately (Costa-jussà, OpenClaw Atlas,
    HiL-Bench, COMET-22) and situates itself in the existing literature.

================================================================================
ADDRESSING POTENTIAL CONCERNS
================================================================================

1. "Is this a product advertisement?"

   No. The reference implementation (Orquor Clinical) is mentioned only as
   a proof-of-concept in Sections 3, 5.4, and Appendix A. The paper's primary
   contribution is the ACTO *category* and methodology. Section 8 explicitly
   states: "We are not pursuing this as a trademark; the category is open.
   The implementations will compete. The category benefits everyone."

2. "Are the benchmark results definitive?"

   No, and we are transparent about this. Section 5.4 states: "These numbers
   are preliminary, drawn from a single-site pilot, and should not be
   interpreted as production guarantees. They are presented here as evidence
   that the ACTO architecture is achievable in practice, not as a competitive
   claim. The intent of this paper is categorical, not commercial."

3. "Does this paper contain medical advice or regulated content?"

   No. The paper describes system architecture and methodology. Regulatory
   sections (Section 6) analyze the *technical primitives* required for
   compliance frameworks, not the compliance frameworks themselves.

4. "Is the author affiliated with a recognized research institution?"

   The author is affiliated with Orquor, a startup. arXiv accepts papers
   from independent researchers and industry practitioners. The paper
   includes an institutional email (research@orquor.com) for verification.
